# Poll Everywhere activities: feedback, frequency response, and Bode plots

These nine multiple-choice questions are organized into three coherent sets. Each set contains three activities and is designed to take approximately 8–10 minutes total, including short peer discussion and instructor debriefing.

For each activity:

1. allow 30–45 seconds for an individual response;
2. if the class is divided, allow 45–60 seconds of peer discussion and repoll;
3. reveal the answer;
4. ask one student to explain why the most attractive distractor is incorrect.

Do not use all nine in one 75-minute meeting. Use the three-question set that matches the day's lesson.

---

## Set 1: Feedback control and applications

### Activity 1.1 — Close the loop correctly

**Recommended placement:** Immediately after deriving the negative-feedback block diagram equations.

**Question**

For a standard negative-feedback system with forward-path transfer function $G(s)$ and feedback-path transfer function $H(s)$, what is the closed-loop transfer function $Y(s)/R(s)$?

**Choices**

A. $G(s)/[1-G(s)H(s)]$

B. $G(s)/[1+G(s)H(s)]$

C. $H(s)/[1+G(s)]$

D. $G(s)H(s)$

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Negative feedback gives $E=R-HY$ and $Y=GE$. Collecting the $Y$ terms produces $[1+GH]Y=GR$, so $Y/R=G/(1+GH)$.

**Misconception targeted:** Using the sign at the summing junction directly as the denominator sign, or confusing the loop transfer function $GH$ with the closed-loop transfer function.

**Debrief prompt:** Which equation in the derivation creates the $1+GH$ denominator?

### Activity 1.2 — Predict the closed-loop response from its poles

**Recommended placement:** After the example $G(s)=K/[s(s+2)]$ and before displaying simulated step responses.

**Question**

A unity-feedback system has

$$
G(s)=\frac{4}{s(s+2)}.
$$

Which description of the closed-loop response is correct?

**Choices**

A. Stable and underdamped, with poles at $-1\pm j\sqrt3$

B. Stable and overdamped, with poles at $0$ and $-2$

C. Unstable because the closed-loop poles are complex

D. Marginally stable, with poles at $\pm j2$

**Correct answer:** A

**Explanation:** The closed-loop denominator is $s(s+2)+4=s^2+2s+4$. Its roots are $-1\pm j\sqrt3$. Their negative real parts imply stability, while the imaginary parts imply an oscillatory transient.

**Misconception targeted:** Treating the open-loop poles as closed-loop poles, or assuming that every complex pole pair is unstable.

**Debrief prompt:** Which part of a complex pole determines decay, and which part determines oscillation frequency?

### Activity 1.3 — Identify the disturbance path

**Recommended placement:** Immediately after introducing an additive disturbance $D(s)$ at the plant output.

**Question**

In a unity negative-feedback system, a disturbance $D(s)$ is added directly at the plant output. Which transfer function maps $D(s)$ to $Y(s)$?

**Choices**

A. $G(s)/[1+G(s)]$

B. $1/[1+G(s)]$

C. $G(s)$

D. $1/G(s)$

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** With $Y=G(R-Y)+D$, collecting terms gives

$$
Y=\frac{G}{1+G}R+\frac{1}{1+G}D.
$$

The disturbance path is the sensitivity function $S(s)=1/[1+G(s)]$.

**Misconception targeted:** Assuming that the reference and output-disturbance signals pass through the same closed-loop transfer function.

**Debrief prompt:** Why does a large loop gain reduce an output disturbance only at frequencies where $|G(j\omega)|$ is large?

## Set 2: Frequency response

### Activity 2.1 — Apply magnitude and phase

**Recommended placement:** Immediately after introducing

$$
y_{ss}(t)=A|H(j\omega)|\cos[\omega t+\phi+\angle H(j\omega)].
$$

**Question**

Suppose

$$
H(j3)=0.4e^{-j\pi/6}
$$

and

$$
x(t)=5\cos(3t+\pi/4).
$$

What is the sinusoidal steady-state output?

**Choices**

A. $2\cos(3t+\pi/12)$

B. $2\cos(3t+5\pi/12)$

C. $5\cos(3t+\pi/12)$

D. $0.4\cos(3t-\pi/6)$

**Correct answer:** A

**Explanation:** Multiply amplitudes: $5(0.4)=2$. Add phases: $\pi/4-\pi/6=\pi/12$. The output frequency remains $3$ rad/s.

**Misconception targeted:** Adding amplitudes, multiplying phases, or replacing the input amplitude with the system magnitude.

**Debrief prompt:** Why does an LTI system preserve the input frequency in sinusoidal steady state?

### Activity 2.2 — Separate transient from steady state

**Recommended placement:** After the complete causal-response example for $H(s)=4/(s+4)$.

**Question**

The zero-state response is

$$
y(t)=[-2.4e^{-4t}+2.4\cos(2t)+1.2\sin(2t)]u(t).
$$

Which term is the transient generated by the system pole?

**Choices**

A. $-2.4e^{-4t}$

B. $2.4\cos(2t)$

C. $1.2\sin(2t)$

D. Both sinusoidal terms

**Correct answer:** A

**Explanation:** The system pole at $s=-4$ produces the $e^{-4t}$ mode. It decays. The input poles at $s=\pm j2$ produce the sinusoidal steady-state terms.

**Misconception targeted:** Calling the entire causal response the steady-state response, or assuming that every term in a zero-state response is forced and therefore permanent.

**Debrief prompt:** What remains after several time constants, and why?

### Activity 2.3 — Interpret nonzero initial conditions

**Recommended placement:** Near the end of the frequency-response lesson, after discussing the assumptions behind $H(j\omega)$.

**Question**

A stable LTI system has nonzero initial conditions and is driven by $A\cos(\omega t)u(t)$. Which statement is correct?

**Choices**

A. Initial conditions change the sinusoidal steady-state frequency.

B. $H(j\omega)$ is invalid whenever initial conditions are nonzero.

C. Initial conditions add a zero-input transient, but the long-term sinusoidal response is still predicted by $H(j\omega)$.

D. The zero-input response becomes part of the transfer function.

**Correct answer:** C

**Explanation:** Nonzero initial conditions add natural-response terms. For a stable system, those terms decay, leaving the sinusoidal steady state determined by $H(j\omega)$.

**Misconception targeted:** Confusing the complete response with the long-term frequency response, or treating a transfer function as a description of stored initial energy.

**Debrief prompt:** What stability assumption is necessary for the initial-condition contribution to disappear?

## Set 3: Bode plots

### Activity 3.1 — Interpret the corner-frequency symbol

**Recommended placement:** Immediately after introducing the normalized first-order factor $1/(1+s/\omega_p)$.

**Question**

For

$$
H(s)=\frac{1}{1+s/10},
$$

what does $\omega_p=10$ mean?

**Choices**

A. The pole is at $s=+10$, and the corner frequency is $10$ Hz.

B. The pole is at $s=-10$, and the corner frequency is $10$ rad/s.

C. The pole is at $s=-0.1$, and the corner frequency is $0.1$ rad/s.

D. The magnitude is always $10$ dB.

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** Since $1/(1+s/10)=10/(s+10)$, the pole is at $s=-10$. The positive number $\omega_p=10$ rad/s is the corresponding Bode corner frequency.

**Misconception targeted:** Confusing the pole coordinate with the positive corner-frequency label, or confusing radians per second with hertz.

**Debrief prompt:** What are the exact magnitude and phase at $\omega=\omega_p$?

### Activity 3.2 — Accumulate Bode slopes

**Recommended placement:** After teaching the pole-and-zero building-block table.

**Question**

Consider

$$
H(s)=\frac{10(1+s/10)}{(1+s)(1+s/100)}.
$$

What is the asymptotic magnitude slope for $10<\omega<100$ rad/s?

**Choices**

A. $+20$ dB/decade

B. $0$ dB/decade

C. $-20$ dB/decade

D. $-40$ dB/decade

**Correct answer:** B

**Explanation:** The pole at $1$ contributes $-20$ dB/decade. The zero at $10$ contributes $+20$ dB/decade. Between $10$ and $100$ rad/s, the contributions cancel, giving zero net slope. The pole at $100$ has not yet changed the slope.

**Misconception targeted:** Counting only the most recent corner or forgetting that slope contributions accumulate.

**Debrief prompt:** What does the slope become just above $100$ rad/s?

### Activity 3.3 — Read a Bode plot as a sinusoidal response

**Recommended placement:** At the end of the Bode lesson, after students have practiced converting decibels to amplitude ratios.

**Question**

At $\omega=5$ rad/s, a Bode plot gives a magnitude of $-6$ dB and a phase of $-30^\circ$. For

$$
x(t)=4\cos(5t+10^\circ),
$$

what is the approximate steady-state output?

**Choices**

A. $2\cos(5t-20^\circ)$

B. $-2\cos(5t-30^\circ)$

C. $4\cos(5t-20^\circ)$

D. $0.5\cos(5t+40^\circ)$

**Correct answer:** A

**Explanation:** A magnitude of $-6$ dB corresponds approximately to a ratio of $0.5$, so the output amplitude is $4(0.5)=2$. Add the system phase to the input phase: $10^\circ-30^\circ=-20^\circ$.

**Misconception targeted:** Treating negative decibels as a negative amplitude or replacing the input phase instead of adding the phase shift.

**Debrief prompt:** Why does the output still have angular frequency $5$ rad/s?
