# 7. Frequency response

## The result

For a stable LTI system driven by

$$
x(t)=A\cos(\omega t),
$$

the steady-state output is

$$
y_{ss}(t)=A|H(j\omega)|\cos\left(\omega t+\angle H(j\omega)\right).
$$

The system changes amplitude and phase, but the steady-state sinusoid keeps the input frequency.

## Where this expression comes from

We can derive it using partial fractions without introducing the term *residue*.

Write the cosine as two complex exponentials:

$$
A\cos(\omega t)=\frac{A}{2}e^{j\omega t}+\frac{A}{2}e^{-j\omega t}.
$$

The corresponding transform contains the input poles $s=\pm j\omega$:

$$
X(s)=\frac{A}{2}\left[\frac{1}{s-j\omega}+\frac{1}{s+j\omega}\right].
$$

Because $Y(s)=H(s)X(s)$, its partial-fraction expansion contains a term

$$
\frac{B}{s-j\omega}.
$$

Find $B$ with the same cover-up method used for every distinct pole:

$$
B=\left.(s-j\omega)Y(s)\right|_{s=j\omega}.
$$

Substituting $Y(s)=H(s)X(s)$ gives

$$
B=\frac{A}{2}H(j\omega).
$$

Therefore the $s=j\omega$ input pole contributes

$$
\frac{A}{2}H(j\omega)e^{j\omega t}.
$$

The conjugate pole contributes the conjugate term. Adding them and taking the real part gives

$$
y_{ss}(t)=A\operatorname{Re}\{H(j\omega)e^{j\omega t}\}.
$$

Finally, write $H(j\omega)=|H(j\omega)|e^{j\theta}$ to obtain the amplitude-and-phase formula.

## Why it is called steady state

The complete causal response also contains terms generated by the system poles:

$$
y(t)=\underbrace{y_{tr}(t)}_{\text{system-pole terms}}+
\underbrace{y_{ss}(t)}_{\text{input-pole terms}}.
$$

If the system is stable, the system-pole terms decay. The sinusoidal input-pole terms remain.

## Important conditions

- $H(j\omega)$ must be finite.
- The system must be stable for the transient to decay.
- A zero of $H(s)$ at $s=j\omega$ can eliminate the steady-state output at that frequency.

## Concept check

If $H(j3)=0.4e^{-j\pi/6}$ and $x(t)=5\cos(3t)$, predict the steady-state output.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$$y_{ss}(t)=2\cos(3t-\pi/6).$$

</details>

## A second derivation using the eigenfunction property

Complex exponentials are eigenfunctions of LTI systems. If

$$
x(t)=e^{j\omega t},
$$

then convolution gives

$$
y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(\tau)e^{j\omega(t-\tau)}d\tau
=e^{j\omega t}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(\tau)e^{-j\omega\tau}d\tau.
$$

The integral is $H(j\omega)$, so

$$
y(t)=H(j\omega)e^{j\omega t}.
$$

The waveform keeps the same frequency because the system only multiplies it by the complex number $H(j\omega)$. The magnitude of that number scales amplitude; its angle shifts phase.

## Worked example

Let

$$
H(s)=\frac{4}{s+4},\qquad x(t)=3\cos(2t).
$$

Evaluate

$$
H(j2)=\frac{4}{4+j2}.
$$

Its magnitude and phase are

$$
|H(j2)|=\frac{4}{\sqrt{4^2+2^2}}=\frac{2}{\sqrt5},
$$

$$
\angle H(j2)=-\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{2}{4}\right).
$$

Therefore

$$
y_{ss}(t)=\frac{6}{\sqrt5}
\cos\left(2t-\tan^{-1}(1/2)\right).
$$

No differential equation or partial fractions are required when only the sinusoidal steady state is requested.

## Frequency-selective behavior

For $H(s)=a/(s+a)$,

$$
|H(j\omega)|=\frac{a}{\sqrt{a^2+\omega^2}}.
$$

Low frequencies pass with magnitude near one, while high frequencies are attenuated. This is a first-order low-pass system. At $\omega=a$,

$$
|H(ja)|=\frac{1}{\sqrt2},
$$

which corresponds to approximately $-3$ dB.

Magnitude in decibels is

$$
20\log_{10}|H(j\omega)|.
$$

## Common mistakes

- Substituting $s=j\omega$ into $X(s)$ instead of the system transfer function $H(s)$.
- Multiplying phases instead of adding them.
- Reporting $\omega$ in hertz. Angular frequency uses rad/s; $\omega=2\pi f$.
- Using the steady-state formula for an unstable system without considering the growing natural response.
- Assuming the formula gives the complete response. It omits transient and zero-input terms.

## Additional practice

2. A stable system has $H(j5)=0$. What steady-state output results from $\cos(5t)$?
3. For $H(s)=1/(s+1)$, does the phase lag increase or decrease as frequency increases?

2. Zero.
3. The lag increases in magnitude: $\angle H(j\omega)=-\tan^{-1}(\omega)$ approaches $-90^\circ$.

</details>
